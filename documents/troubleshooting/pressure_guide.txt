# Troubleshooting Guide: Pressure Surges, Pulsations & Fluid Dynamic Faults

## 1. Operating Pressure Tolerances
- Hydraulic circuits nominal: 25 - 35 bar (Spike tolerance: <= 40 bar for < 200 ms).
- Pneumatic systems nominal: 7 - 9 bar (Differential across filter <= 0.6 bar).
- Dangerous Conditions: Sudden discharge drop with surge oscillations, indicating pump slip, cavitation, or unseated relief valves.

## 2. Common Symptoms & Diagnostic Remedies

### Symptom: High-Frequency Pressure Pulsation (> 50 Hz ripple)
- Root Cause: Worn piston rings, damaged discharge valve reed, or lost nitrogen pre-charge in bladder accumulator.
- Diagnostic Check: Measure accumulator gas bladder pressure when system is de-pressurized. Pre-charge should equal 0.6 to 0.75 of minimum system operating pressure.
- Remediation: Recharge bladder with high-purity dry nitrogen gas; inspect check valves for seating erosion.

### Symptom: Rapid Pressure Drop under Operating Load
- Root Cause: Internal bypass in proportional directional valve or ruptured hydraulic cylinder seal.
- Diagnostic Check: Perform thermographic scan of valve body; localized hot spot (> 10°C delta) indicates internal bypass leakage orifice.
- Remediation: Re-spool valve or replace cartridge seal assembly.

### Symptom: Suction Cavitation & Vane Chatter
- Root Cause: Blocked suction strainer or high oil viscosity during cold startup.
- Remediation: Inspect strainer mesh cleanliness; verify hydraulic oil tank heater is operational during startup regimes.
