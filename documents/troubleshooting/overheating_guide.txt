# Troubleshooting Guide: Thermal Overheating & Heat Dissipation Failures

## 1. Temperature Anomaly Signatures
- Normal Operating Envelope: 45°C to 75°C depending on machine duty cycle.
- Pre-failure Thermal Drift: Steady upward temperature ramp of > 0.5°C/hour under static electrical load.
- Critical Threshold: Casing temperature > 85°C or bearing temperature > 95°C.

## 2. Root Cause Analysis Flowchart

### Excessive Bearing Temperature (> 90°C)
- Possibility A: Over-lubrication (grease churn causes fluid friction and thermal spike within 2-4 hours of greasing).
  - Action: Vent housing relief valve and allow excess grease to purge at running temperature.
- Possibility B: Bearing starvation or degraded lubricant varnish.
  - Action: Check grease degradation, blackening, or acidity. Flush and replenish with fresh ISO-approved lubricant.
- Possibility C: Excessive pre-load or axial thermal binding.
  - Action: Measure floating bearing axial float; check thermal expansion allowances on mounting bracket.

### Drive Motor Stator Overheating
- Possibility A: Restricted cooling airflow / clogged fan cowl.
  - Action: Clean intake shroud and verify fan rotation direction.
- Possibility B: Voltage unbalance > 2% resulting in severe negative-sequence currents and eddy current heating.
  - Action: Measure line-to-line phase voltages at motor terminals under full load.
- Possibility C: Mechanical overload due to downstream process friction or jammed tool spindle.
  - Action: Discouple driven machine and rotate rotor manually to verify mechanical free spin.
