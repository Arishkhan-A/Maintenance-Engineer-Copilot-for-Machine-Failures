# Technical Theory: Industrial Centrifugal Pumps & Fluid Dynamics

## 1. Fundamental Principles & Governing Equations
Centrifugal pumps convert mechanical rotational energy from a driver (electric motor or turbine) into hydrodynamic energy using centrifugal force. Fluid enters axially through the pump suction eye and is accelerated radially outward by the rotating impeller vanes.

### Bernoulli's Equation and Energy Conversion
The conversion of kinetic energy to static pressure follows Bernoulli's principle along a streamline:
P_1 / (rho * g) + V_1^2 / (2 * g) + Z_1 = P_2 / (rho * g) + V_2^2 / (2 * g) + Z_2 + h_loss
As fluid exits the impeller at high velocity, it passes through the expanding cross-sectional area of the volute casing or diffuser vanes, decelerating fluid velocity (V) and elevating static pressure (P).

### Affinity Laws (Scaling Rules)
For a constant impeller diameter (D):
- Flow rate: Q_1 / Q_2 = N_1 / N_2 (Proportional to shaft speed)
- Total Head: H_1 / H_2 = (N_1 / N_2)^2 (Proportional to square of speed)
- Power Absorbed: P_1 / P_2 = (N_1 / N_2)^3 (Proportional to cube of speed)

## 2. Component Architecture & Subsystems
- Impeller: Closed, semi-open, or vortex designs with backward-curved vanes (optimal for head-capacity stability).
- Casing / Volute: Spiral casing engineered to collect liquid discharged from the periphery of the impeller.
- Mechanical Shaft Seal: Cartridge mechanical seal (API 682 Plan 11/53A) preventing pressurized process fluid leakage.
- Shaft & Bearing Assembly: Angular contact thrust bearings (7314 BECBM) paired with deep groove radial bearings (6312) to absorb combined radial and axial hydraulic thrust.
- Wear Rings: Replaceable casing and impeller wear rings maintaining tight clearance (0.25 mm - 0.40 mm) to limit high-pressure recirculation to suction.

## 3. Operating Parameters & Standard Thresholds
- Operating Speed: 1450 to 2950 RPM
- Discharge Pressure: 4.0 to 16.0 bar
- Maximum Bearing Housing Temperature: 82°C (Alarm: 75°C, Trip: 85°C)
- Permissible Vibration Velocity (ISO 10816-7 Category 1 / Group 2):
  - Zone A/B (Good/Acceptable): < 3.0 mm/s RMS
  - Zone C (Alert / High Risk): 3.0 to 5.0 mm/s RMS
  - Zone D (Critical Trip): > 5.0 mm/s RMS
- Minimum Continuous Stable Flow (MCSF): Typically 30% to 40% of Best Efficiency Point (BEP) flow.

## 4. Theoretical Failure Modes & Physical Mechanisms
### Cavitation Dynamics & NPSH Starvation
Cavitation occurs when local static pressure falls below the fluid vapor pressure (P_local < P_vap), causing microscopic vapor bubbles to nucleate at the impeller suction eye. When these bubbles travel into higher-pressure zones along the vane, they collapse violently (implosion pressures up to 10,000 bar and local micro-jet velocities exceeding 1000 m/s), eroding metallic impeller material (pitting) and generating high-frequency acoustic emissions (broadband noise 5 kHz - 20 kHz) and severe suction line vibration.
Condition: Net Positive Suction Head Available (NPSHa) must exceed NPSH Required (NPSHr) by a safety margin of at least 0.5 m to 1.0 m:
Margin = NPSHa - NPSHr >= 0.8 m.

### Mechanical Seal Face Thermal Degradation & Dry Running
Dry running or lack of flush circulation vaporizes the fluid film between rotating silicon carbide and stationary carbon seal faces, resulting in thermal heat checking, blister formation, and elastomer Viton O-ring extrusion.

### Shaft Deflection & Radial Bearing Overload
Operating below MCSF creates internal hydraulic recirculation and rotating stall, causing massive unbalanced radial forces (F_radial = K_r * rho * g * H * D_2 * b_2) that flex the shaft, leading to premature bearing cage spalling and seal leakage.

## 5. Maintenance & Diagnostic Guidelines
- Check suction strainer differential pressure weekly (Delta-P < 0.35 bar).
- Verify dynamic balancing of impeller per ISO 1940-1 Grade G 2.5.
- Monitor mechanical seal flush barrier pressure (maintain 1.5 bar above seal chamber pressure for Plan 53A).
- Measure oil temperature differential across bearing housing cooling jacket.
